## The Threat Has Changed Shape

Cybercriminals are now using AI to identify vulnerabilities, automate phishing attacks, and target businesses that have not updated their defences. SMEs are the fastest-growing target group, and AI has made the attacks cheaper and more convincing than anything that came before.

This is not a future risk. It is the environment UK small businesses are operating in today. And understanding what has changed is the first step to doing something useful about it.

## What AI Has Done to the Threat Landscape

Until relatively recently, a sophisticated cyberattack required real technical skill. Crafting a convincing phishing email, identifying which businesses had specific software vulnerabilities, or personalising an approach enough to fool someone who should know better: all of this took time and expertise.

AI has commoditised most of that.

[Phishing emails](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/phishing) written with AI assistance no longer contain the spelling errors and awkward phrasing that used to make them identifiable. Voice cloning technology means a call from a number that sounds like your accountant might not be your accountant. AI tools can scan publicly available data about your business, your staff, and your suppliers in minutes, then generate a personalised attack at a scale no human team could match.

The cost of running these attacks has collapsed. The volume has increased accordingly.

## Why SMEs Are the Primary Target

There is a common misconception that cybercriminals focus their attention on large organisations. In practice, large enterprises have dedicated security teams, enterprise-grade infrastructure, and legal and compliance functions that specifically monitor for this kind of threat.

SMEs have none of that. They typically have weaker perimeter defences, less staff training, and no one whose job it is to watch for suspicious activity. They also hold data that criminals find valuable: client records, financial details, employee information, and in many cases access credentials to larger organisations they supply or work with.

The phrase used in security circles is that SMEs are "the soft underbelly of the supply chain." An attack on a ten-person professional services firm can be the entry point to the larger client whose data sits in that firm's systems.

## What They Know About You Before They Approach

This is the part that surprises most business owners. Before a targeted attack, criminals are likely to know more about your business than you might expect.

Your website tells them who your clients are, what services you offer, and in many cases who works for you. LinkedIn provides your staff list, their job titles, their career histories, and the names of clients they mention in their activity. [Companies House](https://www.companieshouse.gov.uk) holds your registered details, your filed accounts, and your company structure. Job adverts reveal which software platforms you use. Press releases and case studies tell them who your largest relationships are.

None of this is hidden. It is the same information you publish to win business. But AI can aggregate and analyse it in minutes to build a profile that makes a targeted attack significantly more effective.

## How This Connects to AI Adoption

It would be a mistake to read this as an argument against using AI in your business. The risk of not adopting AI is just as real as the risk of adopting it poorly, and the criminals are not waiting for anyone to catch up.

The connection is this: responsible AI adoption includes understanding the threat environment you are operating in. Businesses that are thoughtful about how they use AI, how they handle data, and how they train their staff are also better positioned to defend against the attacks that use AI as a weapon.

This is why Nucleus AI treats governance and risk as part of every engagement, not an afterthought. Understanding which tools your team is using, how client data flows through your business, and where your current exposure sits is not just a compliance exercise. It is foundational to making good decisions about AI adoption.

## Practical Steps That Make a Difference

You do not need a security team to meaningfully reduce your exposure. The [NCSC's Small Business Guide](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/small-business-guide) covers the essentials, and some of the most effective measures are also the most straightforward: [multi-factor authentication](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/multi-factor-authentication-online-services) across all business systems, a clear policy on what data can go into AI tools and which platforms are approved for use, and staff who have been briefed specifically on current AI-powered social engineering techniques rather than just given a generic phishing awareness session.

The key is that these steps work better when they are part of a deliberate, structured approach rather than a collection of individual decisions made under pressure.

Understanding where you are now is always the right starting point.
